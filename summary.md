# EEG feature benchmark for continuous wrist trajectory decoding

Summary report. All numbers below are regenerated by `make_figures.py` and
`analyze.py` from the benchmark CSVs; none are transcribed by hand.

## Introduction

Continuous wrist trajectory decoding from scalp EEG is limited less by the
temporal model than by what is fed into it. The backbone (UniMamba3-A3) was
fixed by prior work; the open question was which representation of the
32-channel signal it should consume, and how channels should be coupled before
the temporal model sees them.

Two design axes were benchmarked:

- **Axis 1, the feature frontend.** How each (channel, patch) of raw EEG becomes
  a node feature vector.
- **Axis 2, the GCN adjacency.** Which matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{32\times32}$
  defines the graph the spatial mixer aggregates over.

These axes are independent, and a quantity such as PLV can sit on either. It
does not behave the same way on both:

| | Where it enters | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **PLV as node feature** | Axis 1 | A per-channel descriptor inside the token fed to the GCN |
| **PLV as adjacency** | Axis 2 | Replaces the covariance matrix; sets which channels talk to which |

Every claim below is tagged with its axis. A statement about "PLV" with no axis
tag is ambiguous and should be read as an error.

This report is organised chronologically rather than logically, because the
central result is a reversal: an effect was observed, pursued, and dissolved
under control. The order of discovery is the argument.

## Key results

- **The feature ranking is stable across every adjacency tested.** `handfeat`
  (0.806) and `bandpower` (0.800) are first or second in 8 of 9 arms; `raw eeg`
  is last or second-to-last in all 9. Mean pairwise Spearman across arms: 0.78.

- **But the ranking is almost entirely a training-stability ranking, not an
  information ranking.** A feature's mean R² correlates with how often its runs
  collapse at r = −0.98. Conditional on not collapsing, the spread between the
  best and worst feature shrinks from 0.30 to 0.19.

- **PLV as adjacency does not outperform covariance.** Paired over 120 cells,
  `plv_beta − cov` = **−0.004** (p = 0.85). The arms are indistinguishable.

- **The original observation was a run artifact.** The effect that motivated the
  adjacency sweep (`handfeat`, 0.70 → 0.83) was measured against a run in which
  two subjects failed to train. Re-running the *same* graph recovers 0.806, and
  the remaining PLV advantage is +0.024.

- **The pipeline's cell-level replicate noise is ~0.2 R².** Between two runs of a
  numerically identical graph, 23% of cells move by more than 0.10 and 14% by
  more than 0.20. Every adjacency effect measured here is smaller than 0.05.
  **No single-seed cell comparison in this pipeline is interpretable.**

## Pipeline & evaluation protocol

### Fixed chain

Everything outside the two swept axes is held constant:

```
EEG [B, T, 32]
  → frontend        (Axis 1)  → per (channel, patch) feature vector
  → GCN             (Axis 2)  → spatial mixing over the 32-node graph
  → linear lift     → embed_dim = 8
  → UniMamba3-A3    → 2 blocks, d_state = 8, expand = 2, headdim = 8,
                      rope_fraction = 0.5, causal
  → Conv1d head     → wrist coordinate at the anchor sample
```

The spatial mixer is `SimpleGraphConv`:

$$x' = W_{\text{root}}\,x + W_{\text{rel}}\,(A x)$$

with the diagonal of $A$ zeroed. Two properties of this implementation matter
for the discussion below: **$A$ is not normalised** (raw `matmul`, sum
aggregation), and **$W_{\text{rel}}$ is shared across all 32 nodes**. Optional
sparsification (`--adj_threshold`, `--adj_topk`) exists but is off for every run
reported here.

### Signal conditioning

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Dataset | WAY-EEG-GAL, grasp-and-lift |
| Subjects | 12 |
| Channels | 32 |
| Sampling rate | 500 Hz |
| Low-pass | 40 Hz |
| Normalisation | per-trial z-score |
| Patch size | 125 samples (250 ms) |
| Input window | 0.5 s, stride 0.1 s |
| Head window | 2 patches |
| Trials per session | capped at 15 |

### Split and training

Sessions split within subject: 1–6 train, **7 validation**, 8–9 test. One model
per subject; no cross-subject transfer. Adam, `lr = 1e-3`, 80 epochs max, early
stopping with patience 10 on the single validation session.

### Metric

Per test trial, over the anchor points of the streaming prediction:

$$R^2 = 1 - \frac{\sum_i (y_i - \hat y_i)^2}{\sum_i (y_i - \bar y)^2}$$

averaged over trials, then over subjects. **Why $R^2$ and not MSE:** EEG is
z-scored per trial, so output scale is restored from training-set target
statistics rather than being intrinsic. Raw MSE is therefore not comparable
across subjects — a subject with a wider natural range of wrist motion produces
a larger MSE at identical decoding quality. $R^2$ normalises by each trial's own
variance. RMSE is logged as an interpretable per-subject side quantity, never as
the ranking criterion.

### Grid

Fully crossed: **10 features × 9 adjacency arms × 12 subjects = 1080 runs**,
one seed each. The unit of analysis throughout is the *cell*: one
(subject, feature) pair within one arm, n = 120 per arm.

## Benchmark axes

### Axis 1 — feature frontends

All frontends share five frozen `firwin` FIR band-passes of length `patch_size`:

$$\delta\;1{-}4,\quad \theta\;4{-}8,\quad \alpha\;8{-}13,\quad \beta\;13{-}30,\quad \gamma_{\text{low}}\;30{-}40\ \text{Hz}$$

No frontend has learnable parameters; the only trainable layer between frontend
and backbone is the lift to `embed_dim = 8`. The comparison therefore isolates
*what information is extracted*, not how much capacity does the extracting.

| Frontend | What it computes | Why it might carry wrist information |
|---|---|---|
| `raw eeg` | Unfiltered sample at patch centre | Baseline; the backbone does all the work |
| `filtered` | Band-filtered value at patch centre | Phase-preserving; tests a frozen filter bank against a learned patch embedding |
| `bandpower` | $\log_{10}$ mean square per band | Sensorimotor $\mu$/$\beta$ desynchronisation tracks movement onset and speed |
| `linelength` | $\log_{10}$ mean $|\Delta x|$ per band | Amplitude × frequency in one scalar; cheap in hardware, robust to drift |
| `zerocross` | Sign-change rate per band | Dominant-frequency proxy, amplitude-invariant |
| `analytic signal` | $(\mathrm{Re},\mathrm{Im})$ per band | Retains amplitude *and* instantaneous phase, which power-only features discard |
| `handfeat` | All four of the above per band | Tests complementarity vs redundancy |
| `phase-locking value` | PLV against the montage, as a node feature | **Axis 1 use of PLV** |
| `handfeat + plv` | Concatenation | Does phase consistency add to amplitude features? |
| `bandpower + plv` | Concatenation | Same question against the strongest single-family baseline |

Phase is included because band power is a *rectified* quantity: it discards the
sign and timing of the oscillation. For a continuous regression target, timing
may matter, and `analytic signal` and `phase-locking value` are the two ways of
restoring it.

### Axis 2 — GCN adjacency

The starting configuration used a precomputed broadband covariance matrix
(`channel_covariance_32.npz`). Substituting a $\beta$-band PLV matrix appeared to
improve results. That comparison confounds two factors: the **measure**
(amplitude vs phase coupling) and the **band** (broadband vs $\beta$).

| Arm | Matrix | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `cov_orig` | Broadband covariance, shipped `.npz` | The original baseline run |
| `cov` | Broadband covariance, recomputed | Pipeline check against `cov_orig` |
| `cov_beta` | $\beta$-band covariance | Isolates the measure at matched band |
| `plv_beta` | $\beta$-band PLV | The configuration that appeared to help |
| `plv_alpha` | $\alpha$-band PLV | $\mu$ is sensorimotor; partial effect expected if band-specific |
| `plv_delta` | $\delta$-band PLV | **Negative control**: no sensorimotor rhythm |
| `plv_beta_scaled` | $\beta$-PLV, gain matched | Separates the pattern from its scale |
| `abs_cov_beta_scaled` | $|\beta$-cov$|$, gain matched | Tests whether the sign is the problem |
| `uniform_scaled` | Constant off-diagonal matrix | **No connectivity at all** |

`plv_delta` and `uniform_scaled` are the load-bearing arms. Every physiological
account of the effect predicts they fail.

Two hypotheses were stated in advance:

- **H1, amplitude invariance.** Covariance entries scale with channel variance,
  so the graph is dominated by high-power channels rather than coupled ones.
- **H2, band specificity.** `plv_beta` is band-limited while `cov` is broadband,
  so the comparison is not information-matched.

A framing caveat: PLV is sometimes justified as *avoiding volume conduction*.
That is imprecise. PLV is computed from instantaneous phases and remains
sensitive to zero-lag contributions; the estimators designed to suppress them
are imaginary coherency, PLI and wPLI. H1 and H2 are stated in mechanistic terms
instead.

### 2. Swapping in β-band PLV

![Fig 2](fig2_features_plv_beta.pdf)

`handfeat` rises to 0.830 and `bandpower` to 0.815, which is what motivated the
adjacency sweep. Two other movements are larger and were visible at the time:
`analytic signal` rises from 0.534 to 0.694 (+0.160) and `zerocross` falls from
0.654 to 0.380 (−0.274). Both exceed every adjacency effect measured in this
report. That inconsistency, not the `handfeat` gain, is the honest reading of
this figure.

### 3. The distribution is bimodal

![Fig 3](fig3_bimodality.pdf)

Pooling all 120 cells of the covariance arm: **65.8% above 0.70, 13.3% below
0.35, only 20.8% in between.** Runs either converge to roughly 0.85 or collapse
toward zero. A mean R² of 0.508 for `raw eeg` describes no actual subject; it is
approximately (fraction of converged runs) × 0.85.

### 4. Two runs of the identical graph

![Fig 4](fig4_same_graph_runs.pdf)

`cov` and `cov_orig` use matrices that are numerically the same: correlation
0.999976 between the two, maximum absolute element difference 0.0096. For

| Subject | `cov_orig` | `cov` | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1, 2, 6, 7, 9, 10 | 0.80–0.87 | 0.76–0.88 | ≤ 0.08 |
| 3 | 0.712 | 0.865 | +0.153 |

| **12** | **0.285** | 0.807 | **+0.522** |

Subjects 11 and 12 collapsed in the `cov_orig` run and did not in the `cov` run.
Two failures out of twelve move the `handfeat` arm mean by **+0.110**.

The effect originally attributed to PLV was `plv_beta` − `cov_orig` = **+0.133**
for `handfeat`. Against the re-run of the same graph it is **+0.024**.

### 5. The noise floor

![Fig 5](fig5_noise_floor.pdf)

Across all 120 cells, between two runs of the numerically identical graph:

| Threshold | Cells exceeding |
|---|---|
| \|Δ\| > 0.05 | 36.7% (44/120) |
| \|Δ\| > 0.10 | **23.3% (28/120)** |
| \|Δ\| > 0.20 | **14.2% (17/120)** |
| \|Δ\| > 0.30 | 10.8% (13/120) |

Median \|Δ\| is 0.034, but the tail is heavy. The mean difference is +0.014
(p = 0.51), so this contrast **passes as a pipeline check while concealing a
per-cell instability that invalidates cell-level reading.**

## Discussion

### The original effect did not exist

The observation that launched the adjacency sweep — `handfeat` improving from
0.70 to 0.83 when covariance was replaced by β-band PLV — was measured against a
run in which two of twelve subjects failed to train. The same graph, re-run,
gives 0.806. Pooled over all features and subjects, `plv_beta − cov` = −0.004
(p = 0.85).

H2 (band specificity) is rejected: `plv_delta` matches `plv_beta` to within
0.002, and `plv_alpha` to within 0.008. H1 (amplitude invariance) cannot be
evaluated, for the reason given next.

### The conditioning explanation does not survive the matrix statistics

A natural account of `cov_beta`'s deficit is that signed, variance-scaled
matrices make poor graph operators, so any non-negative well-scaled matrix
outperforms them. **The matrices contradict this.** Broadband covariance has the
*most* negative entries (13%) and the *largest* spread (sd 0.275) of any arm
tested, and it sits at the top of the table (0.666). `cov_beta` has fewer
negatives (3%) and less spread (0.214) and sits at the bottom (0.620).
Non-negativity and scale do not order the arms.

**This report does not have an explanation for why `cov_beta` is the lowest
arm.** Its deficit (−0.047 vs `cov`) is the largest adjacency effect measured,
and it is roughly three times the single available estimate of arm-level
replicate noise (0.014). That is suggestive but not decisive, for two reasons:
the replicate estimate comes from a single pair of runs, and the paired test
treats 120 cells as independent when the collapse structure is strongly
subject-driven (subject 3 collapses in 46.7% of its cells, subject 2 in 1.1%).

### What the architecture makes plausible, and what it does not establish

`SimpleGraphConv` applies $A$ without normalisation and shares $W_{\text{rel}}$
across all 32 nodes. Under a uniform $A$, the neighbour term reduces to
$c\,(S - x_i)$ with $S$ the channel sum, so the layer computes a learnable
combination of each channel and the global mean — a learned re-referencing step
rather than graph message passing. This is consistent with `uniform_scaled`
performing at the top of the table.

**It is an interpretation, not a result.** It is not tested here, and it does not
explain `cov_beta`. The cheap decisive experiment is to replace the GCN with an
explicit common-average reference followed by `Linear(20, 8)`: if performance is
unchanged, the layer is doing re-referencing and the adjacency is inert.

### The finding that does hold

The pipeline's cell-level replicate noise is approximately 0.2 R². Between two
runs whose adjacency matrices differ by at most 0.0096, 23% of cells move by more
than 0.10 and 14% by more than 0.20. Every adjacency effect in this report is
below 0.05. **The benchmark, as run, cannot resolve the differences it was built
to measure.**

The probable mechanism is the training protocol rather than the model: a single
validation session (session 7), 15 trials per session, and early stopping with
patience 10. A noisy validation curve on that little data makes the stopping
epoch variable, and an unlucky stop yields an undertrained model and a collapsed
test R². This is consistent with the bimodal distribution in Fig 3, but **it has
not been verified against training logs and remains a hypothesis.**

### Limitations

- **Single seed per cell.** All 1080 runs are one seed. The noise floor is
  estimated from one replicate pair, which gives its magnitude but not its
  distribution.
- **The replicate pair is not a pure seed replicate.** `cov` and `cov_orig`
  differ by a numerically negligible matrix perturbation (max 0.0096) *and* by
  whatever else changed between the two run dates. The source of divergence —
  seed, nondeterminism, or matrix perturbation — is not identified here.
- **The paired tests assume 120 independent cells.** They are 12 subjects × 10
  features, and collapses cluster by subject. Reported p-values are optimistic.
- **The `_c` columns in `adj_controls_summary.csv`** (n = 79–86 of 120, sd
  0.065–0.081 versus 0.22–0.27) restrict to some subset that is not documented in
  the file. Every effect shrinks toward zero on it. It is not used in this report
  because its definition is unknown.
- **The exact definition of the `_scaled` control arms** (what gain is matched,
  and how) is not documented in the delivered CSVs.
- **Within-subject only**, 12 subjects, one dataset, one backbone. Nothing here
  licenses a claim about connectivity in EEG decoding generally; the scope is
  this pipeline.

## Conclusion

The benchmark set out to compare feature frontends and graph adjacencies. It
returns one usable measurement of each, plus one that was not planned.

On features, the ranking is reproducible across nine adjacency arms
(Spearman 0.78) and `handfeat` and `bandpower` lead consistently. But the
ranking tracks collapse rate at r = −0.98, so it ranks training stability more
than extracted information. Conditional on convergence, the features are much
closer together than their means suggest.

On adjacency, no arm separates from any other except `cov_beta`, which is
lowest, and for which this report offers no explanation. A constant matrix
performs as well as β-band PLV, and δ-band PLV — which is itself nearly constant
— performs as well as both. The effect that motivated the sweep does not survive
direct measurement.

The unplanned result is the useful one. A contrast labelled as a routine pipeline
check turned out to rerun a numerically identical graph, which makes it a
replicate. Read at the cell level, it shows 23% of cells moving by more than 0.10
R² and 14% by more than 0.20 — an order of magnitude above the effects the
benchmark was designed to detect.

### Next steps

1. **Multi-seed before anything else.** Three to five seeds per cell, reporting
   the distribution rather than the mean. Nothing else in this list is worth
   doing first.
2. **Diagnose the collapses.** Inspect validation curves for the collapsed cells
   (subjects 3, 5, 8 and 11 are the frequent offenders) to confirm or reject the
   early-stopping hypothesis. If confirmed: widen the validation set, raise
   patience, or restore best weights rather than stopping.
3. **Test the re-referencing interpretation.** Explicit CAR plus `Linear(20, 8)`,
   no GCN. If it matches, the graph layer can be removed.
4. **Only then revisit adjacency.** Under a noise floor of 0.2, no adjacency
   comparison is worth running.
